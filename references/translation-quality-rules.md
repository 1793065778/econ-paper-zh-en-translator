# Translation Quality Rules

## Core Standard

Fidelity comes before elegance. The English should read like a high-quality academic paper, but the Chinese source controls meaning, evidence, sequence, and claim strength.

Fidelity does not mean word-by-word translation. Preserve source meaning and material evidence, not Chinese sentence count, punctuation structure, or enumeration density.

Academic quality also requires readability. Do not preserve Chinese long-sentence structure when it produces English long sentences with stacked clauses.

## Required Preservation

Preserve:

- research question
- sample and data facts
- coefficients and statistical values
- variables and symbols
- model meaning
- identification strategy
- causal strength
- policy claim strength
- section order
- references
- equations
- figure and table labels

## Allowed Handling

Allow small connective or explanatory handling only when English readability requires it and the addition:

- introduces no new fact
- changes no claim strength
- does not add a mechanism or contribution
- is recorded in `翻译审校说明.md`

Allow rhetorical compression when the source section is verbose, especially in abstracts. Compression is acceptable when it:

- preserves all material claims
- preserves all central numerical results
- does not hide uncertainty or causal limits
- does not remove a result that the paper treats as central
- is recorded as a compression decision in review notes

## Prohibited Handling

Do not:

- add research contributions
- add findings or mechanisms
- add robustness tests
- strengthen causal language
- broaden policy conclusions
- translate reference entries unless asked
- alter formulas, variables, statistics, or numeric results
- copy wording from reference papers
- mechanically translate every Chinese sentence into a separate English sentence when the result is verbose
- preserve a long numbered Chinese list in the abstract if the target-journal abstract style calls for synthesis
- turn one Chinese long sentence into one English long sentence by stacking relative clauses, prepositional phrases, and semicolons

## Acronyms

- Spell out the full form at first appearance.
- Use the acronym later if the acronym is established.
- Do not repeatedly restate full forms after first use unless needed for clarity.

## Final Review

Before completion, check:

- every intended source unit is represented
- no source meaning is omitted
- abstract-level compression is documented when used
- sentence readability has been checked, especially for sentences longer than about 35-40 words
- no Chinese text remains in the clean English draft unless intentionally preserved
- all marked protected items remain unchanged
- terminology and acronyms are consistent
