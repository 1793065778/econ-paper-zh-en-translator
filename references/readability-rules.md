# Readability Rules

Use these rules to keep the English manuscript clear, readable, and professionally academic. The goal is not simple or casual English; the goal is precise English that a reader can process without fighting long sentences.

## Core Rule

Do not map one Chinese long sentence into one English long sentence. Split long Chinese sentences into shorter English sentences when this improves readability and does not change meaning.

## Sentence Length

Prefer:

- most sentences: 15-28 words
- technical methods/results sentences: 20-35 words
- rare upper limit: about 40 words, only when splitting would harm precision

If a sentence exceeds about 35-40 words, revise it unless it is a formula definition, sample description, or legally necessary qualification.

## One Sentence, One Main Job

Each sentence should usually do one main job:

- state the research tension
- describe data
- state the identification strategy
- report a main result
- explain a mechanism
- define a variable
- state a policy implication

Do not pack several of these jobs into one sentence.

## Split Patterns

Split Chinese sentences when they contain:

- multiple causal links
- several "and/while/whereas/which" clauses
- a result plus mechanism plus policy implication
- several subgroup findings
- a method description plus sample description plus outcome definition
- long prepositional chains such as "of ... in ... for ... under ..."

Prefer two clear sentences over one polished but overloaded sentence.

## Avoid

- stacked relative clauses
- repeated "which" clauses
- multiple semicolons in one sentence
- noun piles that hide the actor or mechanism
- excessive "respectively" structures
- abstract nouns replacing clear verbs
- strings such as "the impact of the implementation of the construction of..."

## Prefer

- clear subjects and verbs
- result-first sentences in Results
- short bridging sentences when moving between ideas
- active voice when the actor is clear
- passive voice only when the method/result matters more than the actor

## Examples

Verbose:

```text
Using six waves of high-resolution national grid data from 2000 to 2022 and micro-spatial information on 5,055 wind farms, this paper constructs a panel dataset with more than 2.3 million observations and estimates the impact of wind power projects on nationally protected wildlife species richness using a two-way fixed effects model.
```

Readable:

```text
We combine six waves of high-resolution national grid data from 2000 to 2022 with micro-spatial information on 5,055 onshore wind farms. The resulting panel contains more than 2.3 million observations. We use two-way fixed effects models to estimate how wind power projects affect the richness of nationally protected wildlife.
```

Verbose:

```text
Species loss is context dependent, as spatial regulation through nature reserves and high-quality forest habitats can buffer the impact of wind power, whereas cropland, built-up land, and barren land show significant vulnerability.
```

Readable:

```text
Species loss is context dependent. Nature reserves and high-quality forest habitats buffer the impact of wind power. By contrast, cropland, built-up land, and barren land are more vulnerable.
```

## Final Readability Pass

Before completing each batch:

1. Scan for sentences longer than about 35-40 words.
2. Split overloaded sentences.
3. Check that each sentence has a clear subject and main verb.
4. Preserve all key terms, numerical results, and causal limits after splitting.
5. Record any compression or restructuring in review notes when it affects source-to-draft traceability.
